AEO + AI-answer visibility

AEO Watchdog

Runs every week. It checks how AI assistants answer the questions buyers ask, finds the ones where we don't come up, works out why, decides what to do about each one, writes the fix, publishes it to staging, and checks the following week to see whether the answer changed.

  • Two things get measured: being named in an AI answer, and being cited as a source underneath it. Those move independently, so neither one stands in for the other.
  • Three sensing layers: Brand Radar for model-level visibility, a broad SERP and AI-Overview proxy for query breadth, and a directional Claude layer that covers the blind spot the other two have.
  • The question set maintains itself: a curated core plus a long tail that expands and prunes on its own. Discovery runs on the cheaper data layer. Promoting a question into the scarce core set is still my call.
  • Each gap becomes specific work: classified into an action lane, then checked against a live inventory of 596 posts, current schema and real URLs before anything gets written.
  • The backlog has a memory: moves sit in a persistent list where recurrence raises priority. Greenlight sends a job to generation and opens a deduped Linear issue. Dismiss can resurface later. Kill is permanent, and discovery enforces it.
  • The output is finished work, not a to-do list: existing pages get read and updated in place, new pages get built from CMS fields, and a move that isn't a page becomes the actual draft, brief or outreach ask. Generated copy goes through the humanizer and brand rules before it reaches staging.
  • The loop closes: one approval batch per run, then the next cycle checks cited-pool presence, distinct-answer appearance, and whether that specific published change moved the answer.

What stays human: promotion into the core prompt set, final approval, anything involving another person, and disclosed community participation.

Business value: AI answers are a new distribution channel with no obvious way to measure them. This makes them measurable and shortens the gap between spotting a visibility problem and doing something about it.
The hard part: measurement was never it. Plenty of tools will tell you where you're invisible in AI answers. Getting from that list to a published page and back to a measurement is where these projects usually stop.
Competitive intelligence + growth strategy

Weekly competitive intelligence

A Monday morning report, assembled without me, from nine live sources, in Google Drive by 8:15 a.m. ET. AEO Watchdog output is one of the nine. The rest is what competitors and the category are doing, read against our own growth priorities.

  • Every recommendation has to survive one question: does this help signup-to-trial conversion? Installs on their own don't count. Where there is a path, the report spells out the reasoning so leadership can argue with it instead of taking it on trust.
  • Product and funnel changes count as competitive signals: onboarding, first run, signup flow, pricing steps, cancellation changes and funnel architecture often matter more than a feature announcement, because they change the economics rather than the marketing.
  • Nine sources: app-store data and reviews, Meta Ads Library, Reddit, competitor blogs and press pages, App Store and Play release notes, news, social and funding, organic search movement, Apple and Google platform announcements, and AI-answer visibility.
  • Fetching is separate from writing: six scheduled jobs pull the API and scraped data before the report gets assembled, so one slow source doesn't hold up everything behind it.
  • There is a freshness contract: data that is missing, malformed or more than 36 hours old triggers a live re-fetch. A source is only marked unavailable after that second attempt fails, and the report prints the error.
  • What comes out: an executive summary, a competitor scoreboard, deep dives on anything material, category and platform watch, and three to five recommended actions with owners, target dates and a definition of success.
  • It remembers what has gone wrong before: ratings stay separate from written reviews, sample sentiment never gets presented as a store rating, competitor facts are pinned, and a failed fetch never turns into an invented number that looks fresh.
  • It stays decoupled from AEO Watchdog: it reads the Watchdog's finished output as one signal but doesn't run or own it. A broken report can't take out a week of AI-visibility tracking.
Business value: leadership gets a current read on competitors, category shifts and acquisition pressure without anyone rebuilding the research each week, and changes in positioning, product, funnel strategy or paid creative show up early enough to respond to.
The hard part: competitive intelligence usually fails in one of two directions. It collects everything and recommends nothing, or it recommends confidently from stale data. The funnel filter and the freshness contract exist to block one failure each.
Growth analytics + scoring

Signup quality and leadership reporting

Weekly scoring and leadership reporting for a consumer SaaS business with more than 4 million users and 2,000+ signups a day. User-level data at that volume behaves differently from a few hundred enterprise accounts, and the model has to hold up week after week without anyone hand-cleaning a cohort.

  • Every weekly signup cohort gets scored by a multi-signal model tied to observed conversion bands.
  • Activation and winback target lists come out of the same run, so the analysis feeds lifecycle work instead of sitting in a doc.
  • The weekly leadership report keeps several activation definitions apart and labels each one, because they don't mean the same thing.
  • Versioned logic, historical cohorts and anomaly guards stop an unusual week from quietly rewriting the model.
Business value: growth gets a straight answer on who to activate or win back, whether acquisition quality is drifting, and where leadership should be spending attention.
The hard part: definitions. Two people can both say "activated" and mean different things, and at 2,000 signups a day nobody catches that by eye.
Lifecycle + product feedback

Personalized weekly behavior report

A weekly report sent to users, built from data that was never stored as a weekly number.

  • Weekly behavior is derived from the difference between two reliable snapshots.
  • Safe reruns, hashed archival identifiers and freshness checks underneath it.
  • Delivery is spread across the week, and everyone still sees the same completed reporting period.
  • Separate variants for premium, freemium, zero-week and no-session states.
  • Where the value shown is modeled rather than measured, the copy says so and under-claims on purpose.
Business value: users see what the product did for them, which supports retention and creates a natural upgrade or winback moment, without adding another engagement loop of the kind the product exists to reduce.
The hard part: the privacy decision changed the data model, the data model changed what the copy could claim, and the delivery schedule changed both. None of it could be designed in sequence.
Content + SEO/AEO operations

Optimization and landing-page factory

Finds the gaps worth filling, rewrites existing pages, and stages structured new ones without redesigning the page every time.

  • Content Scan and AEO playbooks surface the pages worth rescuing or rewriting first.
  • Humanizer and content plugins bring voice, evidence and anti-AI-tell rules into production.
  • The landing-page workflow separates generated content from the template, which only has to be designed once.
  • New work goes to staging for review before production.
Business value: search and AI-visibility gaps turn into work faster, weak pages that can still recover get priority, and a new high-intent page costs a fraction of what it used to.
The hard part: knowing where to stop automating. The template is worth handing to a machine. Anywhere brand, evidence or publishing risk is involved, a person still reads it before it goes out.
Agent-readable knowledge

OKF bundle + Explorer

A test of whether agent-readable knowledge is worth building. The bundle turns company knowledge into plain files an agent can fetch directly. The Explorer shows what happens when one tries.

  • Built to Google Cloud's Open Knowledge Format v0.1: one concept per Markdown file, YAML frontmatter, organized into product, concepts, playbooks and comparisons.
  • The bundle holds 39 concepts generated from AEO Watchdog research and published as text/markdown for direct retrieval rather than page scraping.
  • The Explorer embeds all 39 and retrieves across both frontmatter and body using keyword overlap.
  • Retrieved concepts appear as cards before the answer is generated, so you can see the evidence it used.
  • The answer is synthesized from those retrieved concepts only, with no outside model knowledge.
  • A weak or empty retrieval is the most useful result: it names a gap in the bundle on the spot.
Business value: company knowledge becomes easier for agents to pull, and the team can find weak or missing concepts before they turn into visibility problems.
The hard part: anyone can claim their content is structured for agents. Very few can show you the retrieval that proves it, and the retrieval is the only part a buyer's model will ever see.
Decision science

Research and diagnostic analysis

Not everything should be a system. Some questions get asked once, and for those the bar is that the source data, the definitions and the reasoning can all be checked afterward.

  • Growth diagnoses separate market demand from distribution changes and funnel effects.
  • Cohort analysis corrects event semantics and right-censoring before anyone draws a conclusion.
  • Recommendations label measured facts, modeled estimates and directional evidence separately.
Business value: money and engineering time stop going to the wrong problem, because a market change, a distribution loss and a funnel break get separated before anyone decides.
The hard part: staying honest about the error bars when the room wants a clean answer. Every one of these ends with the conditions under which the conclusion would be wrong, and that section has been right often enough to keep writing it.

Product & creative AI

Behavior-change product

Focus Coach

Catches somebody at the moment they try to end a session early. It asks what is in the way, then cuts an oversized task down to something they can start in the next five minutes.

  • Product specification and system prompt, with the tone rules built into the behavior.
  • A session debrief loop and seeded user history for the demo.
  • Intervention logic grounded in procrastination research, with a strict no-guilt tone.
Intended business value: better session completion and retention, by making the product useful at the one moment it currently isn't.
Generative audio

Sage: adaptive focus music

A focus-music engine that composes in the browser for the exact length of a work session, develops as the session goes, and resolves when it ends.

  • Composer-directed rules translated into engine behavior.
  • No vocals, no loops you would notice, and nothing that starts abruptly, because it is meant to be forgettable while you work.
  • Working prototype, research brief and an on-device development path.
Intended business value: differentiation and more value per session, from audio that runs on the device with no per-minute streaming cost.
